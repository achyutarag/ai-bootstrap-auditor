\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Statistics for model evaluation.}
Paired bootstrap and significance testing for comparing two systems on the same test items are
long established in NLP~\cite{koehn2004significance,dror2018hitchhiker}, and underpowered
comparisons are common~\cite{card2020power}. For language models, Miller~\cite{miller2024errorbars}
recommends paired differences, clustered standard errors and power analysis, and Bowyer
et~al.~\cite{bowyer2025clt} show that CLT-based error bars fail on benchmarks of fewer than a few
hundred items---the regime our sample-floor and sign-test checks target. \emph{evalci}~\cite{evalci2026}
packages paired tests, confidence intervals, power analysis and multiple-comparison correction for
per-item LLM results; \emph{rliable}~\cite{agarwal2021precipice} provides stratified-bootstrap
intervals for reinforcement-learning benchmarks; and PermRanker~\cite{bakas2026permranker} ranks many
models in medical-imaging challenges with per-case ranks and pairwise permutation tests. These
tools \emph{report} uncertainty for comparisons or rankings; ours turns it into a \emph{release
decision} for one change, and audits that decision procedure itself.

\textbf{Online experimentation.}
A/B-testing practice separates success metrics from guardrails and checks the platform itself
with A/A tests~\cite{kohavi2020trustworthy}. Schultzberg et~al.~\cite{schultzberg2024riskaware}
formalize ``ship if superior on a success metric and non-inferior on every guardrail'', correcting
false positives across success metrics and power across guardrails; our routing of guardrails
outside the false-positive correction follows the same logic. Always-valid inference handles
continuous monitoring~\cite{johari2022alwaysvalid}; our harness currently measures and warns about
the inflation caused by REVIEW$\to$RETEST loops rather than correcting it.

\textbf{Testing statistical and ML software.}
A statistical tool has no per-run oracle: whether one verdict is right is unknown~\cite{barr2015oracle}.
We therefore combine three oracle-free techniques---calibration against synthetic ground truth,
metamorphic relations~\cite{segura2016metamorphic} (e.g.\ a batch of one must agree with a single
audit), and differential testing~\cite{mckeeman1998differential} against closed-form and exact
tests---in the spirit of ML testing~\cite{zhang2020mltesting} and of work on randomness-induced
flakiness in probabilistic software~\cite{dutta2020flaky}. Applied to our own tool, they exposed six
defects that ordinary unit tests had missed.
